Payday checklist

Payday checklist: 7 payments to make the day your salary lands

Most budgets go wrong in the first week, when the salary feels bigger than it is. A payday checklist fixes that: the important money moves on day one, and you spend only what's left.

2 min read · Updated 24 Oct 2026

The checklist

  1. Rent or home paymentBefore anything else.Needs
  2. Emergency fund transferPay your future self first.Emergency
  3. Monthly investmentA halal fund, gold or your own business.Invest
  4. Money for parentsSet the amount once; don't wait to be asked.Family
  5. Health insuranceMonthly, or put aside towards a yearly premium.Health
  6. Electricity, gas and waterClear the bills before late fees start.Needs
  7. Phone and internetThen spend what's left.Needs

Why the order matters

Savings sit near the top on purpose. Take two friends on the same ₹50,000 salary:

Friend 1 spends first

Pays bills, shops, eats out, then saves whatever is left on the 30th.

₹24,000Set aside in a year · about ₹2,000 a month

Friend 2 saves first

On payday moves ₹15,000 to investments and ₹5,000 to an emergency fund, then lives on the rest.

₹2,40,000Set aside in a year · ₹20,000 a month

Same salary; only the order changed. That's what "pay yourself first" means. (Example amounts, returns not counted.)

Set it up once

  • Write the amount next to each line, so payday takes minutes, not an evening.
  • Schedule the transfers for the day after your salary usually lands, so savings leave before you see them.
  • Add a line for yearly bills. For insurance, school fees or renewals that come once a year, put aside a twelfth each month so they don't wreck one payday.
  • Review it twice a year. When rent or bills change, update the amounts.

What's left is yours

After the seven payments, the rest covers groceries, travel and day-to-day spending until next payday. Divide it by the days left and you have a daily limit to check yourself against.
