How to build a 6-month emergency fund

An emergency fund is money set aside for the month that goes wrong: a lost job, a hospital bill, an urgent trip home. With it, a bad month stays a bad month instead of turning into debt. Here's how to size one and fill it on an ordinary salary.

3 min read · Updated 24 Oct 2026

Four steps

  1. Add up one month of needs. Rent or home payment, groceries, bills and transport: the costs that carry on even if your income stops. On a ₹50,000 take-home, the 40% Needs share is ₹20,000.
  2. Multiply by six. That's your target: ₹20,000 × 6 = ₹1,20,000. Use what your needs really cost, not your salary. If six months feels far away, aim for three first.
  3. Save 10% of your salary every month. Move it on payday, before you spend anything: ₹5,000 a month on a ₹50,000 take-home. Treat it like a bill you pay to yourself.
  4. Reach it in about two years. ₹1,20,000 ÷ ₹5,000 = 24 months. Anything extra, like part of a bonus, gets you there sooner.
Emergency fund₹50,000 of ₹1,20,000

Halfway there already covers two and a half months of needs, so the fund starts protecting you long before it's full.

Same rule, any salary

On a ₹60,000 salary, six months of needs comes to ₹1,44,000. Saving ₹6,000 a month gets you there in the same 24 months. With 40% for needs and 10% saved, the timeline stays about two years whatever you earn.

Where to keep it

Somewhere you can reach within a day or two, and apart from your everyday account so it doesn't slowly leak into daily costs. Not in investments: their value can drop just when you need the money. If you avoid interest, a current account or an account with an Islamic bank does the job.

When to use it

  • Yes: losing your job, medical bills, urgent repairs to your home, urgent travel to family.
  • No: sales, holidays, a new phone or wedding gifts. Give those their own saving goal so the fund stays whole.

If you do use it, refill it before you go back to your other goals.

Once it's full

When six months of needs are covered, the 10% is free for your next goal, such as Umrah, a home or investing more. Check the target once a year: if your rent goes up, your fund should too.
