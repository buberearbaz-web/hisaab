How Zakat on savings is worked out, in 4 steps

Zakat is 2.5% of the wealth you have held above nisab for a full lunar year. Knowing the rough amount early lets you plan for it instead of scrambling in Ramadan. This is a simple estimate, not a ruling: confirm your own Zakat with a scholar.

2 min read · Updated 24 Oct 2026

The four steps

  1. Add up your savings. Cash, money in bank accounts, and the gold and silver you own, valued on your Zakat date.
  2. Compare the total with nisab. Nisab is the value of 612.36 g of silver or 87.48 g of gold at today's rate. Scholars differ on which measure to use, so ask yours.
  3. Check it has been a lunar year. Zakat is due once your wealth has stayed above nisab for one full Hijri year.
  4. Give 2.5% of the total you hold on your Zakat date.

What usually counts

  • Usually counted: cash at home, bank balances, gold and silver, and money you've put aside for goals such as Umrah or a home.
  • Usually not counted: the home you live in, your car and the things you use every day.
  • Ask a scholar about: jewellery you wear, investments and shares, business stock, money you owe and money owed to you. Rules on these vary.

Pick a fixed Zakat date

Many people choose one date in the Hijri calendar, often in Ramadan, and do the sum on that day every year. It keeps the lunar-year rule simple, and you know when the money needs to be ready.

Plan for it during the year

Treat Zakat as a payment you can see coming. Once you know roughly what it was last year, put a twelfth of it aside each month, so the full amount is there on your date and doesn't come out of one month's budget.
