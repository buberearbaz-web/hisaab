Salary split

The 40-30-5-5-10-10 salary split, and how it compares with 50/30/20

The 50/30/20 rule is the best-known budget: half for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings. The 40-30-5-5-10-10 split keeps the same idea, giving every rupee a job before the month begins, but builds in an emergency fund, family and your own growth.

The big difference is how much is set aside: 40% (investments plus the emergency fund) instead of 20%. The trade-off is that there's no 30% for wants, so treats and eating out have to fit inside the spending buckets. It's a stricter plan, with family and a safety net taken care of first.

Which one suits you?

  • 50/30/20 if you're just starting out, or rent alone takes close to half your pay.
  • 40-30-5-5-10-10 if you want savings, family support and a safety net handled on payday.
  • Or a mix. The shares are a starting point. If 40% won't cover your needs, start at 50% and move a little each time your salary goes up. Just keep the total at 100%.

Built around halal money habits

Income is salary, business, freelance or rent, with no interest (riba). The 30% goes into halal options such as gold or your own business, 10% goes to parents, relatives and sadaqah so giving comes first rather than from what's left, and Zakat is estimated on what you save.

Start on your next payday

  1. Write down your take-home, after tax.
  2. Multiply it by each share, or let Hisaab do the maths.
  3. Move the saving buckets first. The emergency fund and investments go on payday; you live on the rest.
