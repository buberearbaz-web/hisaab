What to watch at each level

  • ₹30,000, a first salary: the split still works with smaller numbers. ₹3,000 a month to an emergency fund is ₹36,000 by this time next year. Put health insurance first in the Health share.
  • ₹50,000: ₹20,000 a month is saved and invested. If rent pushes needs past 40%, take the difference from Investments for now and keep the emergency fund going.
  • ₹75,000: the emergency fund line alone builds ₹90,000 in a year.
  • ₹1 lakh: ₹40,000 a month saved and invested. The risk here is spending creeping up with your salary, so let raises go to savings first.

Climb the ladder

Because 40% of every salary is saved and invested (30% investments plus the 10% emergency fund), that amount grows with each raise: ₹20,000 a month at ₹50,000, ₹30,000 at ₹75,000, ₹40,000 at ₹1 lakh and ₹60,000 at ₹1.5 lakh.

Working abroad and supporting family back home? Money sent home belongs in the 10% Family share, so it's planned on payday instead of squeezed out at the end of the month.

Make it yours

  1. Use your take-home pay, after tax, not your gross salary.
  2. Check the needs line against your real rent or home payment and bills. If it doesn't fit, change the shares and keep the total at 100%.
  3. Move the emergency fund and investment amounts on payday, before the month's spending starts.
