Money routine

Your monthly money routine: 5 steps and a 10-minute check

Budgets rarely fail on the plan. They fail on the follow-through. This routine takes a few seconds a day, five minutes a week and ten minutes at the end of the month.

2 min read · Updated 24 Oct 2026

The five steps

  1. Log your salary the day it arrives. Until you do, your budget is working from a guess.
  2. Log spending as it happens. It takes a few seconds at the till. Leave it to the weekend and you're guessing again.
  3. Tick off fixed payments like rent or home payment, bills and money for parents. The payday checklist has the order.
  4. Check once a week. Five minutes every Sunday: is each bucket inside its share? If one is running hot, slow it down now, not on the 30th.
  5. Back up on the 1st. Save a copy of your records at the start of every month, so a lost phone doesn't mean a lost year.

The month-end check: 5 questions for the 30th

Ten minutes and a cup of chai, and next month gets easier.

  1. Where did most of my money go? Look at your biggest spending category.
  2. Did I pay myself first? Were savings and investments moved on payday?
  3. Which spend do I regret? Pick one to cut next month.
  4. Am I closer to my goal? Check the month you'll reach it.
  5. What's coming next month? Birthdays, fees, festivals and renewals.

Write the answers down. After three months you'll start to see your own patterns.

Once a year: plan the next one in an evening

One hour and a cup of tea. Here's an example with a KWD 1,000 take-home:

  1. Write your monthly take-home, after tax: KWD 1,000.
  2. List your fixed payments: rent, bills, savings, insurance, family back home.
  3. Split it 40-30-5-5-10-10: KWD 400, 300, 50, 50, 100 and 100.
  4. Pick two saving goals, such as a KWD 2,400 emergency fund (six months of needs).
  5. Put it into your budget, and you're done before 9:00.

One small goal for each bucket beats one big resolution.

Example amounts, not financial advice.
