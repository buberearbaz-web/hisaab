Umrah savings plan, and a Ramadan & Eid fund

Big intentions are easier with small, steady amounts. Here are two plans: a long one for Umrah, and a short one so Ramadan and Eid arrive with the money already set aside.

2 min read · Updated 24 Oct 2026

Umrah: how long will it take?

Pick a target and a monthly amount, and the time follows. With an example target of ₹1,50,000:

Save a monthTime to ₹1,50,000
₹5,00030 months
₹8,00019 months
₹12,00013 months
  1. Get a current quote. Package prices vary with the season, the city you fly from and what's included, so set your own target, with room for visas, local travel and gifts.
  2. Choose an amount you can keep up every month, even an expensive one. Steady beats big.
  3. Keep it separate. A named saving goal stops it being spent on other things.
  4. Check the price every few months and move the target if it changes.

Ramadan and Eid: start 16 weeks early

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 8 February; the moon sighting decides. Saving ₹1,000 a week for the 16 weeks before it gives you ₹16,000, split like this:

CostAmount
Iftar and suhoor groceries₹6,000
Eid clothes₹4,000
Eidi and gifts₹3,000
Sadaqah and fitrana₹2,000
Eid dinner for guests₹1,000
₹1,000 a week × 16 weeks₹16,000

Check the fitrana amount with your local masjid.

Starting later? Use Rajab and Sha'ban

  • Rajab (from about 11 December): open a Ramadan and Eid fund, settle small dues you owe and automate a weekly amount. In one example from Saudi Arabia, SAR 150 a week for 8 weeks is SAR 1,200 by Ramadan.
  • Sha'ban (from about 10 January): work out your Zakat estimate, plan iftar and suhoor groceries, and buy Eid things early.
  • Ramadan (from about 8 February): give Zakat and sadaqah, track iftar spending each week and keep Eid shopping inside the fund.

Example amounts, not financial advice. Dates depend on the moon sighting.
